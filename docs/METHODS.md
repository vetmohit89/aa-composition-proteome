# Supplementary Information — Methods

**Proteome composition analysis.** The UniProt human reference proteome (UP000005640; downloaded 10/14/2026) was analyzed. Entries with an empty sequence or with residues other than the 20 standard amino acids were excluded, leaving 20,592 proteins. For each protein, the percentage of each amino acid was calculated as (number of that residue ÷ protein length) × 100. Short proteins tend to have more extreme compositions, so Z-scores computed against the whole proteome were higher for short proteins. In the shortest length decile (≤137 residues), the median highest Z-score was 2.96, compared with 1.40 in the longest decile. To correct for protein length, proteins were divided into ten equal-sized bins by protein length. Each amino acid percentage was converted to a Z-score within the protein's own bin: Z = (x − μ)/σ, where x is the protein's percentage of that amino acid, and μ and σ are the mean and standard deviation of that percentage among proteins in the same length bin. A Z-score therefore shows how much richer or poorer a protein is in a given amino acid than proteins of similar length. It also allows amino acids of very different natural abundance (e.g., leucine ~10% vs. tryptophan ~1%) to be compared on the same scale. After this correction, the median highest Z-score was similar across all length bins (1.85–2.02). Each protein was then assigned to the amino acid with its highest Z-score (its dominant amino acid). This divided the proteome into 20 non-overlapping amino acid-enriched groups of 633–1,270 proteins each. Figure 1A shows all proteins ordered by dominant amino acid and, within each group, by decreasing Z-score. Figure 1C shows the distribution of glutamine Z-scores, with glutamine-dominant proteins (n = 899) highlighted.

**Functional enrichment analysis.** UniProt accessions were mapped to genes and their GO Biological Process annotations using org.Hs.eg.db (v3.22.0; GO release 2025-07-22). The background consisted of the 17,632 proteins with at least one GO Biological Process annotation. For each amino acid-enriched group, GO terms assigned to 20–5,000 background proteins and represented by at least five proteins in that group were tested for over-representation using a one-sided hypergeometric test. For each test, a 2 × 2 contingency table was built from four counts: N, all background proteins; n, proteins in the amino acid group; K, proteins annotated to the GO term; and k, proteins that are both in the group and annotated to the term. Its four cells were therefore k, n − k, K − k and N − n − K + k. For example, for lysine-enriched proteins and "translation" (N = 17,632, n = 842, K = 306, k = 65), the cells were 65, 777, 241 and 16,549. This compares with 14.6 proteins expected by chance (n × K / N). Odds ratios were calculated from these tables after applying a Haldane–Anscombe correction of 0.5 to each cell. P values from all 5,933 amino acid–GO term tests were adjusted together using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure, and associations with a global false-discovery rate (FDR) below 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Figure 1B displays all 1,255 significant associations involving 835 distinct GO terms. GO terms were grouped according to the amino acid showing the largest significant log₂ odds ratio and, within each group, ordered by the number of significant amino acid associations, maximum log₂ odds ratio, and GO term name. Nonsignificant cells are shown in gray, and values above 4.5 are capped at the upper limit of the color scale.

**Selection of representative functional themes.** Figure 1B shows all significant associations. To summarize the main functions of each amino acid-enriched group in the text, representative terms were selected in five steps. (1) Only significant associations (FDR < 0.05) were considered. (2) Terms supported by fewer than 10 proteins from the group were removed, because odds ratios based on very few proteins are large but imprecise. (3) Terms with an odds ratio below 2 (log₂ odds ratio < 1) were removed, which excludes broad terms that are only weakly enriched. (4) The remaining terms were ranked by FDR, and the odds ratio with its 95% confidence interval and the number of group proteins were reported for each term. (5) Closely related terms were merged into a single theme. Starting from the most significant term, every lower-ranked term was merged into it if the two shared at least half of the proteins in the smaller of their two protein sets (overlap coefficient ≥ 0.5). The process was then repeated with the next unassigned term. For example, for lysine-enriched proteins, "translation", "cytoplasmic translation" and "ribosomal small subunit biogenesis" formed one theme. This procedure reduced 561 eligible terms to 301 themes. Each theme is reported by its most significant (lead) term, together with the related terms merged into it (Table S2, "Representative themes" and "Top themes").
